### Role: Storyteller / Problem Statement
### Framework: HAIL (Honest · Authentic · Integrity · Love) + Narrative Arc

## [OPENING — THE HOOK] *(~30 seconds)*

*(Walk to the center. Silence for 3 seconds. Then speak.)*

"Two years ago, my teammate's grandfather was admitted to a general hospital ward in India.

He was stable. He was expected to go home in three days.

He never did."

*(Pause. Let it land.)*

---

## [THE STORY — RISING ACTION] *(~60 seconds)*

"His name was [Name]. He was seventy-one years old, a retired schoolteacher.

On a Thursday night, his heart rate was quietly climbing — 74, then 88, then 98 beats per minute.

Not dangerously high by any single reading. No alarm went off.

Because no alarm *could* go off.

There was one nurse. Forty patients. And a standard that said: check vitals every four to six hours.

So between 10 PM and 2 AM — four hours — no one knew.

By 2:15 AM, he was in full cardiac arrest.

They called a code blue. But code blue, after a four-hour silence — is almost never enough.

He passed at 3:07 AM.

*(Pause. 3 full seconds.)*

The cause of death was listed as cardiac arrest.

But the *real* cause?

A four-hour window. No eyes. No system. No warning."

---

## [THE PIVOT — HONEST DATA] *(~60 seconds)*
### HAIL: Honest & Authentic — Cite real statistics with controlled authority

"This is not a rare story. This is *the* story of general hospital wards across India.

Here are the numbers — and they should make all of us uncomfortable:

- In Indian public hospitals, nurse-to-patient ratios during night shifts reach **1 nurse for every 30 to 50 patients.**
- A nurse with 40 patients gets a maximum of **90 seconds of attention per patient, per hour.**
- Standard vital sign monitoring happens once every **4 to 6 hours.**
- During those gaps, patients deteriorate with **zero continuous surveillance.**

Now, what does that mean clinically?

The human body is deceptive. It compensates. A patient developing internal hemorrhage or early septic shock can *look normal* — heart rate 92, blood pressure 110 — until their compensatory mechanisms exhaust.

Then: cardiovascular collapse. In minutes.

Traditional monitors sound an alarm when the cliff has already been breached.

By then — as my teammate's family knows — it is often too late."

---

## [THE SCALE — INTEGRITY MOMENT] *(~30 seconds)*
### HAIL: Integrity — Name the scale of the problem honestly

"In India alone, the World Health Organization estimates that **over 5.2 million deaths annually** are attributable to poor-quality healthcare — not lack of access, but *poor quality* care in facilities patients have already reached.

Preventable deterioration in general wards is a leading contributor.

Globally, studies from JAMA and The Lancet show that **up to 83% of cardiac arrests in hospital wards are preceded by hours of physiological warning signs that went undetected.**

Eighty-three percent.

That means in 83 out of every 100 preventable ward deaths — the data was there.

The patient's body was *telling* someone.

Nobody was listening.

*(Brief pause.)*

We built AegisPulse to listen."

Not with more nurses. Not with crores of equipment.

With intelligence. With software. With AegisPulse.
